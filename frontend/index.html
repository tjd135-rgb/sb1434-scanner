    SB 1434 Scanner · Analyst Intel

    Everything you need to interpret what the map is showing, from the statute to the DOR use codes to the golf-course ring test.

    1 What is SB 1434?

    SB 1434 is Florida's Infill Redevelopment Act, codified at §163.2525, F.S. It creates a streamlined pathway for redeveloping underutilized parcels sitting on environmentally impacted land.

    The statute applies only in Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties — the three counties in Florida that meet its combined population and municipality thresholds. The intent is to incentivize turning brownfield and contamination-adjacent sites into productive infill development instead of pushing new construction farther out.

    Not the same as the Live Local Act. LLA targets attainable housing on commercial/industrial land regardless of environmental status. SB 1434 targets infill redevelopment on environmentally compromised land — brownfield areas and DEP cleanup sites. The two overlap on some parcels but the underlying tests and unlocked entitlements are distinct.

    2 What makes a parcel qualify?

    A parcel must pass all five gates below to appear on the map.

    1. Gate 1 — Size (≥ 5 acres). Computed as LND_SQFOOT / 43,560 ≥ 5. Small infill parcels don't make the statute's density unlock economic.
    2. Gate 2 — Tri-county. county_fips ∈ {23 Miami-Dade, 16 Broward, 60 Palm Beach}.
    3. Gate 3 — Environmental trigger (either/or). A parcel qualifies if either:
      • Trigger B: centroid lies inside a designated FDEP brownfield area polygon, or
      • Trigger A: centroid lies within 1,500 ft of a DEP cleanup-site point.
      The env_trigger field records which matched (brownfield_area / cleanup_site / both).
    4. Gate 4 — Adjacent to residential. A parcel with a residential DOR use code (001-009) sits within 500 ft. Almost every South-Florida infill parcel meets this, but a handful deep inside a port or a heavy-industry district fail.
    5. Gate 5 — Not excluded. Parcel must not be:
      • Agricultural (DOR 050-069),
      • A government-owned public park (DOR 082 + owner name contains "CITY OF", "COUNTY OF", etc.),
      • Within ¼ mile (1,320 ft) of a military installation identified in §163.3175(2) — Homestead ARB, USSOUTHCOM Doral, USCG Miami Beach/Sector Miami/Ft Lauderdale, FL ANG Homestead Armory.
      The utility check (parcel currently owned by an FPL/Duke/TECO-style entity, or use code 091-097) is a flag, not an exclusion — the 15-year lookback needs manual title research.
    3 Understanding the 13 pathways

    Every qualifying parcel is classified into one of 13 redevelopment pathways based on DOR use code (and, for golf, on the ring test). Order in the CASE matters — more specific codes win over broader ones.

    Pathway 1 · Golf, ringed

    DOR 038, ≥ 80% of perimeter is single-family residential. The SB 1434 overlay applies, constraining density. Less valuable for redevelopment because you inherit the overlay's density cap.

    Pathway 1B · Golf, partially ringed

    DOR 038, 40-79% SF-residential perimeter. Overlay likely applies but the exact boundary computation deserves a manual review of the parcel's actual footprint before you commit.

    Pathway 2 · Golf, not ringed ★

    DOR 038, < 40% SF-residential perimeter. Overlay does not apply — full density flexibility. These are the highest-value deals in the entire dataset. Markers are drawn 30% larger and outlined heavier so they pop on the map.

    Pathway 3 · Industrial

    DOR 041-049. Large footprints frequently sitting on documented contamination — the classic brownfield-to-mixed-use play. Environmental remediation cost is the primary underwriting question.

    Pathway 4 · Commercial

    DOR 011-029 (minus office/auto/fuel below). Strip centers, big box, regional malls sitting on or near brownfield land. Retail apocalypse supply meets residential demand.

    Pathway 5 · Office

    DOR 017-019. Underperforming suburban office on qualifying land. Post-COVID vacancy plus SB 1434 unlock = teardown-to-rental plays.

    Pathway 6 · Institutional

    DOR 070-079 excluding government. Schools, churches, hospitals on qualifying sites. Often long-tenured owners — patient capital deals.

    Pathway 7 · Residential redevelopment

    DOR 001-009 on 5+ acres. Mobile-home parks and aging garden apartments where an SB 1434 unlock supports densification.

    Pathway 8 · Utility

    DOR 091-097, or a utility owner name. 15-year title lookback required before you can rely on the qualification — parcels previously owned by a public utility as defined in §366.02 are excluded by statute. Treat as a flag, not a green light.

    Pathway 9 · Auto/fuel

    DOR 028, 048. Gas stations and auto-repair sites — near-certain petroleum-contamination signal. Cleanup cost is the deal.

    Pathway 10 · Hospitality

    DOR 039. Aging hotels/motels on qualifying land — historically dry-cleaner exposure on-site, plus soft demand at older properties makes these attractive teardowns.

    Pathway 11 · Vacant commercial

    DOR 010. Empty commercial parcels inside brownfield or cleanup zones — least-encumbered path to a clean start.

    Pathway 12 · Mixed use

    DOR 030-035. Existing mixed-use sites eligible for intensification under the statute.

    Pathway 13 · Other

    Everything that passed all five gates but doesn't match a specific pathway bucket — worth eyeballing individually.

    4 The golf-course ring test

    Golf courses split into three sub-pathways depending on their surroundings, and the ring test is what decides which.

    For each DOR 038 qualifying parcel, the scanner:

    1. Approximates the parcel boundary as a circle of equivalent area (r = √(acres · 4046.86 / π)) — we have the centroid + acres but not the actual polygon.
    2. Walks 12 bearings (every 30°) at a radius of the parcel + 200 ft.
    3. At each sample point, KNN-looks up the nearest other parcel and checks whether its DOR use code is single-family residential (001-009).
    4. Computes the percentage of sample bearings that hit residential and classifies:
      • ≥ 80% → ringed → Pathway 1 (overlay applies).
      • 40-79% → partially_ringed → Pathway 1B (review).
      • < 40% → not_ringed → Pathway 2 (no overlay, top deals).

    Why it matters: the SB 1434 overlay caps redevelopment density when a course is ringed by SF residential — to protect the character of those neighborhoods. When the course is not ringed, no overlay, so the unlocked density is unconstrained. This makes not-ringed golf courses the most valuable deals in the entire dataset.

    Per-bearing detail (bearing angle, nearest DOR code, distance to that parcel) is stored in ring_test_samples JSONB and returned on the GET /qualifying-parcels/{parcel_id} detail endpoint. Use it to figure out which side of the course is the unringed edge.

    Caveat. The circular approximation misses course shape detail — long/narrow layouts (fitting around lakes) will have samples that are inside the course itself on some bearings. Anything hovering near the 40% or 80% threshold deserves a visual spot-check in the aerial-view link.

    5 Understanding the environmental triggers

    Gate 3 is the heart of SB 1434 — the environmental trigger is what distinguishes this statute from every other density unlock. Two independent data sources, either one qualifies.

    Brownfield Area (Trigger B)

    FDEP-designated brownfield areas are zones where environmental cleanup is occurring or planned under Ch. 376. They're relatively few (624 loaded statewide) but each covers a substantial footprint. If your parcel's centroid is inside any of these polygons, Trigger B is satisfied — this is the cleanest possible qualification.

    Cleanup Site (Trigger A)

    Individual DEP contamination/cleanup site points from the DEP Contamination Locator Map. 2,363 sites in the tri-county area. A parcel qualifies if its centroid is within 1,500 ft of any one of these points — proximity, not containment.

    Both

    When env_trigger = both, the parcel sits inside a brownfield area and is near a cleanup site. Belt-and-suspenders qualification.

    Environmental triggers are what make SB 1434 unique. This isn't a housing unlock over commercial land; it's a density unlock specifically for environmentally impacted land. Every parcel on the map is by definition contaminated or contamination-adjacent — cleanup cost is always part of the underwriting.

    6 How to use this scanner
    1. Start with the top deals. Set Pathway to 2 · Golf, not ringed ★. There are currently 6 parcels in this bucket — begin your review there.
    2. Narrow to your market. Add a County filter. Miami-Dade is the deepest inventory; Broward and Palm Beach are smaller pools but often cleaner titles.
    3. Click any marker to open the detail popup. The badges at the top summarize pathway / env trigger / UDB status / ring test / adjacency / utility flag at a glance. The Google Maps aerial link opens the exact parcel in satellite view — always use it before you spend more time on a deal.
    4. For golf courses, read the ring-test percentage. Anything between 35% and 45%, or 75% and 85%, is a borderline classification — don't treat the pathway label as gospel until you've looked at the actual boundary.
    5. For Pathway 8 (utility), stop and order a 15-year title history before proceeding. Any prior ownership by an FPL/Duke/TECO-style utility disqualifies the parcel under §163.2525(4)(e).
    6. Use the environmental-trigger filter to split brownfield-area parcels (more established cleanup programs, better-defined liability) from cleanup-site parcels (may need Phase II ESA to confirm contamination extent).
    7. For Miami-Dade, check udb_status. Parcels outside the UDB are still qualifying under SB 1434 but face additional entitlement friction — county comprehensive-plan overrides, potentially. Not a dealbreaker but a friction point to price in.
    8. Export your shortlist. Use the API directly for CSV: GET /qualifying-parcels?pathway=…&county=…. Every field visible in a popup is in the JSON response.
    7 Field glossary
    Parcel ID
    DOR-assigned unique identifier (folio number in Miami-Dade, PCN in Palm Beach). Zero-padded to the county's canonical length.
    DOR Use Code
    Florida Department of Revenue 3-digit land-use classification. Drives every pathway assignment. Reference: DOR Use Codes PDF.
    Acres
    Computed from LND_SQFOOT / 43,560. This is DOR's land area, not building footprint.
    Pathway
    One of 14 values (13 pathways plus pathway_golf_pending, the transient state before the ring test runs). See §3 above.
    Environmental Trigger
    Which Gate 3 source qualified the parcel: brownfield_area, cleanup_site, or both.
    Brownfield Area Name
    If env_trigger includes brownfield_area, this is the name of the FDEP area the parcel sits inside.
    UDB Status
    Miami-Dade only. inside / outside of the county's Urban Development Boundary. Non-Miami-Dade rows are always null — no UDB applies.
    Ring Test Result
    Golf courses only. ringed (≥80%) / partially_ringed (40-79%) / not_ringed (<40%). Non-golf rows are null.
    Ring Test Pct
    The exact percentage of the 12 sample bearings that landed on SF-residential parcels.
    Adjacent Residential
    true if any parcel with DOR 001-009 exists within 500 ft. Required to satisfy Gate 4.
    Utility Flag
    true if the current owner name or DOR use code suggests utility ownership. Not an exclusion — requires 15-year title lookback for the final determination.
    Owner Name
    Current owner from DOR NAL. Truncated to 100 chars in the API response.
    8 Data sources & currency
    • DOR NAL parcels — Florida Department of Revenue Name-Address-Legal file, 2025 vintage. 2,342,443 parcels loaded across Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach. Refresh cadence: annual (DOR publishes in Q3).
    • Parcel centroids — FL Statewide Parcel Centroid FeatureServer (Esri Living Atlas). Joined to NAL via zero-padded parcel_id per county. Coverage is ~63-99% depending on county (loses some condo sub-units).
    • FDEP Brownfield Areas — DEP OpenData Layer 0. 624 designated areas statewide (all 3 counties covered).
    • DEP Cleanup Sites — DEP Contamination Locator Map (Environment MapServer Layer 1). 2,363 tri-county points loaded (filtered from a ~10k-statewide dataset).
    • Miami-Dade UDB — county GIS export. Currently a polygonized-from-line approximation; may be refined against the county's authoritative polygon in a future release.
    • Military installations — 6 tri-county installations per §163.3175(2): Homestead ARB, USSOUTHCOM Doral, USCG Base Miami Beach, USCG Sector Miami, USCG Station Fort Lauderdale, FL Army NG Homestead Armory. Point-buffered footprints; ¼-mile exclusion buffer computed at screen time via ST_DWithin.
