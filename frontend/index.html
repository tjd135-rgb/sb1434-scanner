How It Works · The Statute

Everything you need to interpret what qualifies and why. If you want a workflow for using the tool, see the Scanner Guide.

1 What is SB 1434?

SB 1434 is Florida's Infill Redevelopment Act, codified at §163.2525, F.S. The bill creates a streamlined pathway for redeveloping underutilized parcels sitting on environmentally impacted land.

The problem it solves

South Florida has thousands of acres of contamination-adjacent land — old gas stations, industrial sites, dry-cleaner corridors, brownfields — sitting idle because cleanup costs and regulatory uncertainty make redevelopment uneconomical. Meanwhile the region faces a housing and commercial supply shortage. SB 1434 is a targeted incentive to bring those parcels back into productive use.

How the mechanism works

Parcels that qualify get access to streamlined permitting, potential density bonuses, and reduced regulatory barriers for infill redevelopment. The exact bundle of unlocks depends on the pathway (see §3 below) — a not-ringed golf course is treated very differently from an industrial site inside a brownfield area.

Who benefits

  • Developers who can pair environmental remediation with new construction get a cost/entitlement edge that made deals uneconomic without the unlock.
  • Municipalities get blighted or contaminated land converted to tax-generating use — infrastructure served but under-taxed for decades.
  • Communities get investment in neglected neighborhoods, often around long-standing environmental eyesores.

Geographic scope

Only Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach — the three counties in Florida meeting the statute's population and municipality thresholds.

Relationship to other Florida statutes

SB 1434 is distinct from — though sometimes complementary with:

  • Live Local Act (SB 102/CS/SB 328). LLA unlocks residential density on commercial/industrial land for attainable housing regardless of environmental status. Some SB 1434 parcels also qualify under LLA, but the triggers are different.
  • Bert Harris Act. Property-rights / compensation statute — unrelated mechanism, occasionally invoked when SB 1434 unlocks butt against local downzoning.
  • FDEP Brownfield Redevelopment Act (§376.77-.86). The broader statutory framework that creates FDEP brownfield areas in the first place. SB 1434 uses those designations as a Gate 3 trigger.
2 The five qualification gates

A parcel must pass all five to appear on the scanner. Each gate covers a specific piece of the statute.

Gate 1 · Minimum 5 acres

The test: LND_SQFOOT / 43,560 ≥ 5.
Why the statute has it: SB 1434 targets meaningful redevelopment sites — infill assemblage of individual lots isn't what the bill was written for.
Data: DOR NAL land square footage.
Edge case: Parcels at exactly 5.0 acres are borderline — DOR's LND_SQFOOT can round; verify against the county PA record before committing.

Gate 2 · Tri-county location

The test: county_fips ∈ {23 Miami-Dade, 16 Broward, 60 Palm Beach}.
Why: The statute was drafted for South Florida's specific environmental and development context.
Data: DOR NAL CO_NO field.

Gate 3 · Environmental trigger (either/or)

The heart of SB 1434 — this is the gate that makes it different from every other density unlock. Two qualifying paths, either is sufficient:

  • Trigger B: Inside an FDEP brownfield area. These are formal cleanup zones designated by FDEP under §376.80. Being inside one of these polygons is the cleanest possible qualification signal.
  • Trigger A: Within 1,500 ft of a DEP cleanup site. Individual contamination points from the DEP Contamination Locator Map — gas stations, dry cleaners, industrial spills, petroleum leaks. Proximity, not containment.

Data: 624 FDEP brownfield-area polygons + 2,363 DEP cleanup-site points (tri-county subset of a ~10k statewide dataset).

Gate 4 · Residential adjacency

The test: at least one parcel with DOR use code 001-009 (residential) within 500 ft.
Why: the statute requires the site to be near existing residential so the infill serves an established community — not a greenfield play.
Data: DOR NAL use codes on all neighboring parcels.
Caveat: DOR use codes don't capture every residential use — condo sub-units, mixed-use residential, etc. If the scanner flags a parcel as "no adjacency detected", verify via aerial before dismissing it.

Gate 5 · Exclusions

Certain land uses are explicitly protected from this redevelopment path.

  • Agricultural (DOR 050-069). Excluded outright.
  • Government-owned public parks (DOR 082 + govt owner name).
  • Military buffer — within ¼ mile (1,320 ft) of any installation listed in §163.3175(2). Applies via ST_DWithin on installation polygons.
  • Institutional (DOR 070-079) — schools, churches, hospitals, cultural orgs. Not actionable as redevelopment targets at scale.
  • Utility (DOR 091-097 or utility-company owner). The 15-year title lookback the statute requires isn't automatable, so these are excluded rather than flagged.
  • Broad government ownership (COUNTY OF, CITY OF, STATE OF FLORIDA, UNITED STATES, SCHOOL BOARD, WATER MANAGEMENT) — unless the DOR use code is commercial or industrial (010-049), in which case the parcel might be leasable and still actionable.
  • Military owner patterns — parcels whose owner name contains AIR BASE / AIR FORCE / MILITARY / NAVY / ARMY / NATIONAL GUARD / COAST GUARD, catching cases that geometric buffering missed.
3 The 13 redevelopment pathways

Every qualifying parcel is classified into one of thirteen pathways based on current DOR use code (and, for golf, the ring test). The pathway shapes the redevelopment thesis.

Golf pathways (1, 1B, 2)

Pathway 1 — Golf, ringed. Course surrounded by single-family homes on 80%+ of its perimeter. The SB 1434 overlay applies and constrains density. Redevelopment plays here are limited to repositioning (banquet, wellness, event, resort) rather than full residential conversion — the overlay caps what you can build.

Pathway 1B — Golf, partially ringed. 40-79% SF-residential perimeter. Overlay likely applies but the classification is sensitive to how you measure the boundary — order a survey and re-run the perimeter analysis before committing.

Pathway 2 — Golf, not ringed ★. Under 40% SF-residential perimeter. Overlay does not apply — full density flexibility. These are the highest-value deals in the dataset because the SB 1434 unlock stacks on top of existing golf-course-residential entitlements.

Pathway 3 — Industrial

DOR 041-049. Warehouses, light manufacturing, packing plants, open storage. Large footprints frequently sitting on documented contamination — the classic brownfield-to-mixed-use play. Environmental remediation cost is the primary underwriting question; request a Phase II ESA before LOI. Zoning-change risk is real: neighborhoods that grew up around industrial often organize hard against conversion.

Pathway 4 — Commercial

DOR 011-029 (excluding office/auto/fuel). Strip centers, big box, community shopping centers, regional malls. Retail apocalypse supply meets residential demand. Verify tenant tails — a long-dated NNN kills the teardown timing — and shopping-center reciprocal easement agreements can lock density for decades.

Pathway 5 — Office

DOR 017-019. Underperforming suburban office on qualifying land. Post-COVID vacancy plus SB 1434 unlock creates a teardown-to-rental play. Verify current occupancy and lease tails; medical office (018) has stickier tenants than plain professional (017).

Pathway 7 — Residential redevelopment

DOR 001-009 on 5+ acres. Mobile-home parks and aging garden apartments are the main pool here. Existing residential entitlement plus SB 1434 unlock supports meaningful densification. Mind tenant-displacement rules — Florida mobile-home-park closures require 6+ months notice and relocation assistance.

Pathway 9 — Auto/fuel

DOR 028, 048. Gas stations and auto-repair sites. Near-certain petroleum contamination on the pad and along historical UST (underground storage tank) footprints. Cleanup cost IS the deal — expect a Phase II ESA and a full remediation estimate before LOI.

Pathway 10 — Hospitality

DOR 039. Aging hotels and motels — often on-site historic dry-cleaner exposure, plus soft ADR at older properties makes teardown competitive with reposition. Union or brand constraints can complicate exit.

Pathway 11 — Vacant commercial

DOR 010. Empty commercial parcels inside brownfield or cleanup zones — least-encumbered starting point because there are no tenants to relocate. But vacant commercial in a brownfield zone often means the last owner walked away because remediation was uneconomic — read the FDEP file before pursuing.

Pathway 12 — Mixed use

DOR 030-035. Existing mixed-use eligible for intensification. Check current FAR utilization vs. what SB 1434 unlocks — sometimes the existing envelope already burns the entitlement.

Pathway 13 — Other

Uncategorized — passed all five gates but doesn't match a specific pathway bucket. Eyeball the DOR use code and current use before deciding whether to pursue.

4 The golf-course ring test

Why golf courses get special treatment. Golf courses in South Florida were platted decades ago inside (or as the anchor of) single-family subdivisions. When you convert a ringed golf course to housing, you're fundamentally changing the character of a residential neighborhood that grew up around it — so the statute imposes an overlay that caps redevelopment density on ringed courses. Un-ringed courses (open on multiple sides, or adjacent to commercial/industrial) don't create the same character problem, so the overlay doesn't apply and full density flexibility is available.

What the test measures. For each qualifying golf-course parcel:

  1. Approximate the parcel boundary as a circle of equivalent area (r = √(acres·4046.86/π)) — we have the centroid and acreage but not the actual polygon.
  2. Walk 12 sample bearings around a ring 200 ft outside the approximated boundary.
  3. At each sample point, look up the nearest other parcel and check whether its DOR use code is single-family residential (001-009).
  4. Percentage of SF-residential hits drives the classification.

Classification thresholds:

  • ≥ 80% → ringed. Overlay applies. Pathway 1.
  • 40-79% → partially_ringed. Overlay likely applies but shape-dependent. Pathway 1B.
  • < 40% → not_ringed. Overlay does not apply — top deal. Pathway 2.

What "partially ringed" means for an investor: it's a borderline classification driven by our 12-sample approximation of a circular boundary on a course that may be long or narrow. Get a boundary survey and re-run the perimeter analysis on the real footprint before treating the parcel as either overlay or non-overlay.

5 Environmental triggers explained

Brownfield area (Trigger B)

An FDEP-designated zone where cleanup is planned or under way under §376.80. These are formally recognized by the state, cover a substantial footprint (typically dozens to hundreds of acres each), and are usually driven by a local government initiative. If your parcel's centroid is inside one of these polygons, Trigger B is satisfied — this is the cleanest qualification signal because the state has already acknowledged the contamination and the community has bought into cleanup.

Cleanup site (Trigger A)

An individual contamination point from the DEP Contamination Locator Map. Gas stations, dry cleaners, industrial spills, petroleum leaks, small chemical incidents. These aren't zones — they're specific pinpoints. A parcel qualifies if its centroid is within 1,500 ft of any one of these points (proximity, not containment). The 1,500 ft radius is the statute's test — it's meant to capture parcels that are meaningfully affected by an adjacent contamination event even if the parcel itself isn't the source.

Both

Some parcels sit inside a brownfield area and near a cleanup site — the belt-and-suspenders qualification. Common for old industrial corridors where the state designated a brownfield area because of the individual cleanup points inside it.

The scanner flags presence, not severity

Neither trigger tells you how bad the contamination is or how expensive cleanup will be. A parcel near a former dry cleaner might have $50k of PCE plume remediation, or it might have $5M of soil-vapor abatement. Every SB 1434 deal requires contamination-severity diligence — pull the FDEP file, commission a Phase I ESA, budget for a Phase II if warranted.

Why the statute uses two triggers

Brownfield areas are the state's formal recognition mechanism, but designations lag. A newly-discovered spill might not get an area designation for years. Trigger A (proximity to individual cleanup sites) closes that gap and lets the statute reach parcels that are affected in practice even without formal zone designation.

6 Field glossary
Parcel ID
DOR-assigned unique identifier (folio in Miami-Dade, PCN in Palm Beach). Zero-padded to the county's canonical length.
DOR Use Code
Florida Department of Revenue 3-digit land-use classification. Drives every pathway assignment. Reference: DOR Use Codes PDF.
Just Value (JV)
DOR-assessed total market value including improvements. Not sale price — but a decent proxy for scale.
Land Value
DOR-assessed value of the land component only. Compare to JV to derive the land/improvement ratio.
Land/improvement ratio
lnd_val / (jv − lnd_val). Ratio ≥ 1 means the land is worth as much or more than what's built on it — the classic underutilized-parcel signal. Ratio of 999 = vacant / near-vacant (no measurable improvement).
Acres
Computed from LND_SQFOOT / 43,560. Land area, not building footprint.
Pathway
One of 12 stable pathways (plus pathway_golf_pending, the transient state before the ring test runs).
Env trigger
Which Gate 3 source qualified the parcel: brownfield_area, cleanup_site, or both.
UDB status
Miami-Dade only. inside / outside. Not an SB 1434 exclusion — a friction indicator (parcels outside face additional county entitlement hurdles).
Ring test result
Golf courses only. ringed / partially_ringed / not_ringed. See §4.
Adjacent residential
Whether any parcel with DOR 001-009 exists within 500 ft. Passing Gate 4.
7 Data sources & currency
  • DOR NAL parcels — Florida Department of Revenue Name-Address-Legal file, 2025 vintage. 2,342,443 parcels loaded across the three counties. Annual refresh (Q3 publish cadence).
  • Parcel centroids — FL Statewide Parcel Centroid FeatureServer (Esri Living Atlas). Joined to NAL via zero-padded parcel_id per county.
  • FDEP Brownfield Areas — DEP OpenData Layer 0. 624 designated area polygons statewide.
  • DEP Cleanup Sites — DEP Contamination Locator Map. 2,363 tri-county points loaded from a ~10k-statewide dataset.
  • Miami-Dade UDB — county GIS export (polygonized from the county's line-string publication).
  • Military installations — six tri-county installations per §163.3175(2). Point-buffered footprints; ¼-mile exclusion at screen time.

The screen is only as fresh as the last run. After a NAL or brownfield/cleanup refresh, hit POST /admin/run-screening to rebuild qualifying_parcels, then POST /admin/run-ring-test to reclassify golf.

Scanner Guide · Using This Tool

Practical workflow, keyboard tips, and how to get the most out of every panel. For the underlying statute see How It Works.

1 Ten-minute workflow
  1. Hit the gold Golf Opportunities tile at the top of the page. Instant filter to Pathway 2 (not-ringed golf) — the highest-value bucket where the SB 1434 overlay does not apply.
  2. Narrow by county if you have a target market. Miami-Dade has the deepest inventory; Broward and Palm Beach are smaller pools but often cleaner titles.
  3. Set Min land/imp ratio ≥ 1.0 (or check Land-heavy only). This surfaces parcels where the land is worth as much or more than what's built — the classic underutilized-parcel signal.
  4. Click a marker to open the detail drawer. Skim the gate checklist, redev angle, and next steps. Every panel is scrollable independently — the map stays put.
  5. Toggle Satellite basemap when scanning a cluster. OSM hides parcel context; satellite shows what's actually there.
  6. Star parcels as you go. The star icon in each card and detail drawer adds the parcel to your watchlist (stored locally in your browser).
  7. Add short notes to your watchlisted parcels. The note field appears in the detail drawer once a parcel is starred.
  8. Toggle Watchlist in the top bar to filter the map/list to just your starred parcels.
  9. Download Watchlist as CSV when you're ready to review offline or share.
2 Reading the map

Marker color = pathway (see legend, bottom-right of the map).
Marker size = √acres, so a 400-acre course looks 4× the diameter of a 25-acre parcel.
Gold star markers are Pathway 2 golf-not-ringed — 30% larger with a heavier outline so they pop.

Floating legend is clickable — clicking any pathway entry filters the map to that pathway.

Satellite toggle in the filter bar swaps OSM streets for Esri World Imagery. Streets give you addresses and street names; satellite shows parcel context — buildings, greenspace, adjacent uses.

Brownfield polygon overlay: when you click a parcel with an env_trigger that includes brownfield_area, the FDEP area outline appears as a gold semi-transparent shape. It clears when you close the drawer.

3 Reading the detail drawer

Click any parcel (map marker or list card) to open the detail drawer on the right. Everything you need to underwrite the parcel is one scroll.

  • Property type (top): the actual current use from the DOR use code — Warehouse, Golf Course, Shopping Center, etc.
  • SB 1434 pathway (just below, color-matched): the redevelopment classification the scanner assigned.
  • Star icon: click to add to watchlist. Note field appears when starred.
  • Parcel ID chip + Copy: the ID you paste into the county property-appraiser search.
  • Value context: JV, land value, and land/improvement ratio.
  • Gate checklist: every one of the five statutory gates with a ✅ / ℹ️ / ⚠️ icon. Green = passed cleanly, blue = data-quality note (verify manually), yellow = borderline.
  • Redevelopment angle: one-line thesis for the pathway.
  • Next steps: per-parcel actionable checks.
  • Action buttons: Google search, 🛰️ Aerial (satellite), 🚗 Street View, County Appraiser deep link.
4 The watchlist

The watchlist is a lightweight shortlist stored in your browser's local storage. No account, no cloud, no sharing surface — it persists across sessions on this device only.

  • Add a parcel: click the ☆ icon on any card or in the detail drawer. Star fills in ★.
  • Add a note: with a parcel starred, a note input appears in the detail drawer. Notes save on every keystroke (debounced).
  • Filter to watchlist: click the Watchlist chip in the top bar. The map and list narrow to just your starred parcels. Click again to clear the filter.
  • Export: click Download Watchlist to grab a CSV of just your starred parcels (with your notes appended).
  • Remove: click the ★ icon again to unstar.

Where it lives: localStorage["sb1434.watchlist.v1"]. Clearing browser storage wipes it — copy anything important out to your own spreadsheet as insurance.

5 Tips by pathway

Golf courses (Pathways 1, 1B, 2)

Only Pathway 2 (not ringed) is unconstrained. For 1 and 1B the overlay caps density; if you own or optioning one, the play is repositioning (banquet, wellness, event space) rather than pure conversion.

Industrial (Pathway 3) & Auto/fuel (Pathway 9)

Assume contamination. Phase II ESA and a remediation estimate before LOI. Cleanup cost varies by 1-2 orders of magnitude between clean industrial and gas-station plumes.

Commercial (Pathway 4) & Office (Pathway 5)

Retail apocalypse plus post-COVID office vacancy. Verify tenant tails — a 10-year Wal-Mart NNN kills your teardown timing.

Residential redev (Pathway 7)

Mobile-home parks and aging garden apartments. Mind Florida tenant-displacement statutes — mobile-home park closures require 6+ months notice plus relocation assistance.

Vacant commercial (Pathway 11)

Least-encumbered starting point — no tenants to relocate. But vacant commercial in a brownfield zone often means the last owner walked away because remediation was uneconomic. Read the FDEP file before pursuing.
